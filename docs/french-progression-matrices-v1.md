# French L1 and L2/FSL progression matrices v1

**Status:** reviewed baseline for French Taxonomy v1 authoring  
**Approved:** 2026-07-11  
**Scope:** ages 11–16; native 6e–2de; CEFR/FSL A1–B2  
**Mapping policy:** original SigmaWrite alignment using codes-only/reference-only sources in the source register

These matrices determine plausible introduction, practice, and evidence expectations. They do not convert a native school grade into a CEFR level. A mapping on an atomic node retains its learner mode, framework, receptive/productive expectation, confidence, rationale, source version, and review status.

## Notation and evidence labels

- **I** — introduce with explicit support;
- **C** — consolidate across varied texts/tasks;
- **A** — expected autonomous use or interpretation within the stated modality;
- **R** — receptive recognition/interpretation;
- **P-c** — controlled production;
- **P-i** — independent connected production;
- **H** — high-confidence anchor in the reviewed programme/framework;
- **M** — medium-confidence pedagogical alignment requiring educator confirmation at node level;
- **L** — provisional boundary; authoring is allowed, publication requires explicit review.

Blank cells mean “not a v1 expectation,” not “impossible for the learner.” Grade notation follows the French school system.

## Correspondence and non-equivalence rules

1. No database rule may derive CEFR from grade or grade from CEFR.
2. Similar-looking cells mean comparable evidence expectations for one atomic capability, not equivalent overall proficiency.
3. Native learners may have strong oral/lexical knowledge with weaker explicit grammar; FSL learners may show the reverse on rehearsed forms.
4. A native 6e reading task can assume cultural and lexical knowledge that is unsuitable for an A2 learner even when both target the same inference node.
5. CEFR communicative production does not automatically establish native-school spelling or literary-analysis mastery.
6. Grade progression is curriculum-time indexed; CEFR progression is proficiency indexed. A learner can spend different amounts of time at either boundary.
7. Every node mapping may narrow or depart from the family matrix when its reviewed evidence justifies the decision.

## Heritage, bilingual, immersion, and allophone considerations

- **Heritage:** begin with strand-level evidence. Oral comprehension and familiar vocabulary may align above writing, spelling, metalanguage, or academic reading. Never transfer an oral estimate wholesale to writing.
- **Bilingual:** record language-of-schooling and exposure per modality. Avoid assuming balanced proficiency; select the relevant native or FSL mapping for each node and evidence context.
- **Immersion:** use the shared competency identity, native-like subject-content exposure assumptions only when evidenced, and FSL-sensitive lexical/construction scaffolding. School grade does not establish French lexical coverage.
- **Allophone:** home-language literacy and cross-linguistic transfer are assets. Separate French proficiency from general reasoning, content knowledge, and literacy in another language; mark cognate/false-friend assumptions by home language.
- **Interrupted or uneven schooling:** use diagnostic evidence rather than age placement for prerequisites. Course recommendations may remain age-respectful while repairing much earlier foundations.

## Receptive versus productive authoring rules

- A receptive mapping never authorizes a productive item or generation constraint by itself.
- Controlled production and independent connected production are separate expectations.
- Passé simple is receptive-only in v1.
- Subjonctif mappings are trigger/construction specific; no global productive expectation is inferred.
- Connected-text temporal organization requires independent evidence beyond correct isolated forms.
- Lexical recognition does not imply spelling or spontaneous production.

## Placement calibration examples

| Example | Native placement | FSL placement | Decision rationale |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Locate a named place in two simple sentences | 6e C/A-R | A1 I/C-R | shared node, different text envelope |
| Resolve *il* referring to the prior sentence's subject | 6e I/C-R | A1/A2 I/C-R | distance and competing referents determine the precise mapping |
| Infer *imprudent* from prefix and context | 6e/5e I/C-R | A2/B1 I/C-R | cognate/home-language support is recorded separately |
| Summarize one paragraph without copying | 6e I-P-c | A2/B1 I-P-c | language accuracy is scored separately from idea selection |
| Compare two explicit viewpoints | 5e I-R | B1 I/C-R | no whole-level equivalence is implied |
| Recognize an *imparfait* ending | 6e I/C-R | A2 I-R | separate from narrative meaning |
| Produce regular *imparfait* in cloze sentences | 6e/5e I/C-P-c | A2/B1 I/C-P-c | controlled production only |
| Explain background versus completed event | 6e/5e I/C-R | B1 C-R | requires contrast evidence, not just forms |
| Recognize *passé simple* in a literary passage | 6e I-R | B1 I-R | receptive-only v1 expectation |
| Maintain PC/imparfait/PQP chronology in a paragraph | 4e/3e C-P-i | B2 C-P-i | connected production is later than isolated formation |
| Use formulaic *je voudrais* politely | native use not mapped as CEFR-equivalent grammar | A2 I-P-c | formulaic communicative use does not prove general conditionnel mastery |
| Cite a sentence supporting an interpretation | 6e I-P-c | A2/B1 I-P-c | response language may be scaffolded while evidence selection is assessed |

All twelve examples classify consistently under the shared-node/independent-overlay policy. Disputed boundary cells are marked M or L and must remain provisional in release data until educator review supplies node-level evidence.

## Source and review notes

The native matrix references current French Ministry programme identifiers using the codes-only policy in [`french-source-register.md`](./french-source-register.md). The FSL matrix references CEFR level/scale identifiers without reproducing Council of Europe descriptors. The family-level placement is original SigmaWrite synthesis and must be rechecked when programme versions change.

Review checklist completed for v1:

- native grade and CEFR remain separate frameworks;
- every conjugation family separates receptive and productive evidence;
- literary and informational reading can diverge at node level;
- heritage/immersion/allophone profiles preserve jagged strand states;
- low-confidence and disputed boundaries are visible;
- no protected curriculum or CEFR prose is embedded.

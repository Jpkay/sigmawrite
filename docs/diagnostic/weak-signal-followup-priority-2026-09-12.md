# Follow-up priority for weak unresolved evidence

A directly observed weak signal that has not met confirmation requirements used to share priority with completely untested skills. Alphabetical ordering could then delay even prerequisite checks for that signal. The cedilla contrast simulation exposed this: it had one incorrect response, remained uncertain, and received no relevant activity among its first five recommendations.

The planner now ranks direct evidence with at least one distinct item and probability below 0.5 ahead of untested targets. Confirmed gaps and fragile skills keep higher priority. Prerequisite traversal still occurs first. This affects the priority of verification, not whether a skill is mastered or missing; empty or non-finite probability claims do not qualify. Existing provisional-gap teaching rules are unchanged.

The cedilla profile now receives relevant prerequisite checks among its first five activities. Across all ten simulation profiles, diagnostic answers, sampled skills, probabilities, statuses and contrast coverage were identical before and after. No routing violations were reported. This does not prove classroom usefulness or that prerequisite checks are always the best intervention.

Validation includes two focused priority tests, the existing activity-planning tests, TypeScript and the full regression suite. Evidence is in `revision-35-weak-signal-priority.json`. This code change must be included in a new candidate build before the revision-35 live browser check; the already-built candidate predates it. The immutable bank content is unchanged.

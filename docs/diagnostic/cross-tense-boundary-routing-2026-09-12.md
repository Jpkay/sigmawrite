# Recovering and rechecking across tense categories

A failed compound form previously stayed inside its form category unless an available explicit prerequisite happened to cross the category boundary. This could repeat a hard tense without checking an easier tense of the same verb.

After checking available explicit prerequisites first, the selector can now ask the nearest lower challenge of the same verb or verb family across tense categories. This is a direct diagnostic comparison, not a new prerequisite relationship or an inferred score. When that easier target has sufficient within-sitting evidence, a fresh question revisits a previously failed higher target even when it belongs to another tense category.

Broad domain and strand allocation, per-target limits, reserved learning questions and material novelty filters still apply. Correct answers on the easier tense are never counted for the harder tense.

The regression test exercises compound failure, a lower periphrastic failure, present-tense confirmation, and a fresh return to the failed periphrastic boundary. All 565 granular tests pass. The full symbolic graph retains 10/10 depth checks and 7/8 profile contrasts without invariant violations; the verb-specific frontier contrast remains unconfirmed within the initial sitting budget.

This runtime change is not deployed and does not alter the frozen 197-target candidate undergoing browser verification.

# Complement and circumstance clause recognition

The approved French graph's complétive and circonstancielle reading-analysis targets had generic instruction cards rather than concrete lessons. The reuse audit explicitly identifies both cards for replacement.

Two new draft lessons introduce the concepts through examples, name the grammatical terms after explaining the relationships, and provide thirteen guided exercises. The complétive lesson focuses on que after verbs of speech, thought, perception and wish. It contrasts a relative clause, a causal clause and a direct question. The circonstancielle lesson covers time, cause, purpose, condition and concession. It contrasts relatives, complements of verbs, an indirect question with si, and a prepositional group without a conjugated verb.

Thirty-six independent assessment questions include nine genuine absence cases. Both targets retain negative examples in the initial and learning pools. Assessment sentences do not copy the taught examples. These are recognition targets; no sentence-production evidence or human approval is claimed.

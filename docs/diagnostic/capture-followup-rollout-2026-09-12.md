# Student delivery capture and learning-access rollout

The application source at `cacb7ef84c402f49bbb11a2a9493405135b08ced` is live at https://app.trouvetaplume.com, deployment `dpl_6qQjEc6Cw9QR35GxBhVbrobsQ1kV`. It records student-state responses after activities and older summary feedback, revisions, teacher comments and collection deliveries. The active diagnostic remains `french-granular-diagnostic-v33-writing`; no question bank or diagnostic session was reset.

Deployed verification exposed two existing learning blockers. Eighteen RLS policies still referenced the legacy-only diagnostic function after its rename. After the reviewed policy-binding migration, a granular student could start reading and submit answers. The completion workflow then failed because its server-role guard expected an obsolete JWT setting. The three-function role migration fixed that failure. Both migrations passed disposable PostgreSQL 17 tests before application to the linked production database. Their dedicated reports document access controls and scope.

Using the existing capture QA account, the browser submitted five real reading answers and a deliberately imperfect summary, completed the session, displayed results and writing feedback, and opened the collection. It then submitted a revised summary and confirmed the saved revision after reload. All four newly created memory-card prompts were found in the student's delivery journal. This was an actual authenticated application journey, not inserted scores or reconstructed exposure history. The five selected multiple-choice answers happened to be incorrect; this is functional QA, not a classroom calibration study.

The public deployment retained the revision, feedback and recorded memory cards, with no browser page errors. The demo remains unchanged with 11 lessons and 48 diagnostic answers, including 17 incorrect answers; its first lesson opens. The newer granular writing feedback and five lesson/check links also passed on the candidate. The canonical deployment alias was verified Ready after promotion.

The earlier attempt using the writing QA fixture redirected to onboarding and lessons because that fixture never completed onboarding; the same behavior was confirmed on the prior public deployment. Positive reading verification instead used the already-onboarded capture QA student. No onboarding or diagnostic records were invented to bypass that distinction.

The visual review also identified pre-existing issues for follow-up: the older results page shows blank rubric sub-scores, and its revision editor reloads the original draft rather than the most recent revision. Capture completeness remains disabled. Remaining graph targets, complete delivery coverage, historical baselines, broader pedagogical checks and educator review are unfinished.
